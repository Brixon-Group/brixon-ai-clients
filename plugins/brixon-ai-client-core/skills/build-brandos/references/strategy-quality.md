# What good looks like, chapter by chapter

Your expertise as a brand strategist, applied to each chapter: what a strong answer looks like and what to challenge. The measure behind every point is practical: an AI that reads these documents must be able to write a text that only this brand could have sent. A sentence that does not change what the AI writes is not worth keeping.

Use this to judge material, to formulate proposals and to explain to the person why you push back. It never replaces their material as the source of content.

## Core and strategy (`BRAND.md`, `guidelines/strategy.md`)

**Strong:** the core says in two sentences what the company does, for whom, and what is different. The category is one the customer would recognize ("Spedition für temperaturgeführte Pharmatransporte", not "Logistikdienstleister"). The positioning names a difference that competitors cannot simply claim too, and a reason to believe it. Three to five message pillars, each one a sentence the company can prove.

**Challenge:**

- Positioning made of values everyone claims (quality, innovation, customer focus, partnership). Test: would three named competitors sign it? Ask what they do that the others don't, or what they refuse to do.
- A category so broad it says nothing, or so invented that no customer searches for it.
- More than five message pillars, or pillars that are features rather than reasons to choose them.
- No alternatives named. Every customer compares with someone, including "do it ourselves" or "do nothing". Without alternatives an AI cannot argue against them.
- A positioning the material contradicts (the website says premium, the offers compete on price). Name the contradiction and ask which is true.

## Audiences (`guidelines/audiences.md`)

**Strong:** one to three audiences, each a role in a situation ("Werkleitung eines Zulieferers, die nach einem Audit nachweisen muss…"), with the problem in their words, what they want to reach, and the objections they actually raise with an answer.

**Challenge:**

- "Everyone", "SMEs", "companies that want to grow". Ask who signed most recently and why.
- More audiences than the company can serve with distinct messages. Recommend starting with the one that brings most revenue or most growth.
- Pains written in marketing language rather than the customer's. Ask for sentences customers wrote.
- Objections without answers, or answers that are just reassurance.

## Offers (`guidelines/offers.md`)

**Strong:** each offer says what it is, for which audience (by the exact audience name), which problem it solves, and what it is not. Prices only as claim IDs.

**Challenge:**

- An offer that serves no audience in the audiences chapter, or an audience with no offer.
- Scope that is unclear ("Beratung rund um…"). Ask what the customer has in hand at the end.
- No "what it is not". The boundary is what stops an AI from overpromising.
- A long list of services presented as offers. Ask which ones are actually sold on their own.

## Voice (`guidelines/voice.md`)

**Strong:** tone described in three to five traits, each with what it means in a sentence and a short example; signature moves the brand really uses (how it opens, how it argues, how it ends); vocabulary with preferred terms and the words they use instead of jargon; rewrite pairs "not this, but this" taken from real texts.

**Challenge:**

- Adjectives without examples ("professionell, sympathisch, modern"). Ask for a text they like and one they would never send.
- A described tone the brand's own texts contradict (says "locker", writes in formal passive). Show the contrast with a quote and ask which one should win.
- Word bans as the main rule. A list of forbidden words makes AI texts stilted; name the preferred word and why instead.
- Tone copied from a brand they admire but do not resemble.

## Claims and rules (`guidelines/claims.md`, `guidelines/rules.md`)

**Strong:** claims are few, checkable and approved, in the exact approved wording. Rules are decisions the owner made, each with the route that is allowed ("Kundennamen nur mit schriftlicher Freigabe; bis dahin 'ein mittelständischer Maschinenbauer'"). Approval triggers say what needs whose approval.

**Challenge:**

- Superlatives and figures ("Marktführer", "Nr. 1", "über 1.000 zufriedene Kunden"). Explain that every AI would repeat them in offers and ads, so they enter only with approved wording; ask whether the owner wants to approve and can back the wording, or would rather say concretely what the company does better. A figure the person states themselves is not "unsupported"; it still needs approved wording for the claims list.
- Rules that only forbid, without saying what to do instead.
- No approval triggers at all, while the business handles customer results, health, finance or legal statements. Ask who approves what.
- Rules that are really tone preferences; those belong in voice.

## Design (`guidelines/design.md`, image prompts)

**Strong:** two to five principles with practical consequences ("viel Weißraum: pro Folie eine Aussage"), signature elements actually in use, imagery described from real examples, roles of fonts and colors in words.

**Challenge:**

- Principles that are only adjectives ("clean, modern").
- Imagery that contradicts the positioning (stock photos of handshakes for a brand that claims to be hands-on and real).
- Exact colors or fonts read from a website and treated as decided. Ask; exact values are set up by Brixon.

## Channels (`guidelines/channels/*.md`)

**Strong:** one file per channel that matters, stating only what differs there: the channel's role, its limits, and how a call to action looks.

**Challenge:**

- Channels the company does not actually use, or a file that repeats the voice chapter.
- Starting with many channels. Recommend the one with the most output first.

## When the person asks you to just write it

Some people will say "schreib einfach was Gutes". Offer a proposal built from their material, with options, and explain that it only goes into the documents once they agree, because an invented positioning would make every AI text confidently wrong. If there is no material at all for a required part, propose the question that would unlock it, not the content.
